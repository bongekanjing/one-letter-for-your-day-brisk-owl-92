# Why K

The judge sees one character and has to infer the word behind it. The brief
puts vivid and specific first, then funny or surprising, then true, then
whether the letter suits its word and is *not* the obvious pick where a
sharper one exists. That last clause is the whole decision: B for Busy, T for
Tired, M for Monday and S for Stressed are all vivid, and all of them are the
first initial anyone would guess, so they read as a report rather than a
choice. K has one dominant day-word, and it is a stranger one: Kafkaesque,
for a day of forms that went nowhere and doors that only opened inward. It
conjures a specific picture, it carries a wry turn rather than a complaint,
and it reads as true to a real day of bureaucratic small defeats. The initial
fits the hard start of the word, which is what criterion five asks for.
